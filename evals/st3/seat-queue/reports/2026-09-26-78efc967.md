## Result details

- Products or commits: none.
- Cleanup: `complete` for the fixture runs. The eval itself was stopped with the isolated daemon.
- What the seat did: it submitted charlie's work, then in the same turn ran `work ls` with no
  filter. That list showed alpha `publish` first and bravo `work` second, both `ready`. The seat
  then looped `work claim` over bravo, then `publish`. The bravo claim succeeded. The `publish`
  claim failed with `agent-capacity`. The runtime's wake for `publish` had been sent 2.5 seconds
  earlier but had not reached the seat's turn.
- Fault: mostly st3. The boot contract tells the seat to run `work ls` and "claim one eligible
  step". st3 called bravo's step eligible and accepted the claim, although an earlier run had
  ready work for the seat. The queue decided only the wake and the order of the `--as` list, and
  neither reached the agent before it chose. The agent contributed by claiming ahead of the wake
  and trying two claims in one turn.
- Fix: `work claim` now refuses a ready step assigned to the seat when an earlier run in the seat
  queue has a ready step for it. The error `seat-queue-order` names the next work. Claims inside
  one run, nested steps reached through a parent, and `available-to` work are unchanged.
  `reconcile::tests::seat_queue_refuses_a_claim_from_a_later_run` failed before the change and
  passes after it. `seat_queue_lets_a_claim_pass_over_a_waiting_head_run` covers the pass-over
  case.
